# strategies/bull_bear/ -- bull vs bear debate

> Nested `CLAUDE.md`; project-wide rules are in the root `CLAUDE.md`. Paths are relative to `src/trading_agent_framework/` unless they start with `tests/`, `scripts/` or `docs/`.

## Architecture

- `bull_bear/` (`BullBearStrategy` in `agent_bull_bear.py`, registered as `"bull_bear"`): lumibot's bull vs bear debate over cross_momentum's ranking, once a week (Tuesday 12:00 ET, `sleeptime = "1D"`, `BullBearParams.rebalance_weekday` = 1: every other session returns at once; a Tuesday whose review completed is not run again after a restart, unlike cross_momentum). Code ranks the universe with `strategies/common/scoring.py` and cross_momentum's own `CONFIG` (never duplicated) and builds the candidates (`debate_set.py`): the top `candidate_count` (15) plus the holdings ranked up to `retention_rank` (35), minus the stocks in their re-entry cooldown (`max_volatility` gates new entries only: the pipeline scores a holding with a copy of `CONFIG` that lifts it, so `scoring.py`/cross_momentum are untouched; a holding ranked worse, unranked or still in cooldown is a forced exit, sold by code, never shown to the agents, and logged as a warning (`forced exit: <symbol> (<reason>)`) just before its sell order is sent: not when the review is abandoned before execution, since nothing is sold then). The fact sheet (`fact_sheet.py`) has NO `held` or `weight_pct`: no agent is told what is held. Beside the momentum figures it carries `return_1m_skip_1w_pct` (the month before the last week: `compute_return_from_prices(closes, 16, 5)`, so a stock that ran up this week is told apart from one that was already strong) and the SEC fundamentals (`fundamentals.py`: `SecFundamentals`, growth, margins, cash flow and debt, money in billions of the REPORTING currency labelled `currency`, a figure that cannot be computed left out), read by code for EVERY candidate as of `strategy.clock.now()` (never the wall clock) through the one `SecEdgarClient` `initialize` builds with `default_client` and shares with the researcher's SEC tools (framework 1-day cache). **Fundamentals count only filings dated BEFORE the review day**, in every mode: company facts carry `filed` as a date only (read as midnight), so `SecFundamentals` reads the statements (and the staleness test) as of the start of `as_of`'s calendar day (in its own timezone) minus 1 microsecond; a 10-Q filed on the review day, often after the close, stays hidden (in paper/live a same-morning filing shows up the next day). The client call still gets the real `as_of` (cache freshness only, no `max_age_days`). The researcher's SEC tools still use `filed <= as_of`, so a tool call can show a same-day filing that the fact sheet does not. The first backtest downloads each debated stock's company facts once (about 4 MB each, several hundred MB of cache in total); later runs and reviews reuse the files. Any SEC failure, or an exception from the injected `fundamentals_of`, gives that stock `fundamentals_unavailable` (the reason; `"error"` for an unexpected exception, with a WARNING naming the symbol) and never abandons the review; `fact_sheet_row` stays pure (the pipeline reads, the sheet merges the fields after `sector`). Four agents hand over through submit tools validated by `HandoffRecorder` (`handoff.py`: `submit_note`, `submit_bull_case`, `submit_bear_case`, `submit_verdicts`; no `from __future__ import annotations`): a researcher run PER STOCK (`search_news` when `research_news` is on, plus the SEC `get_income_statement` (with cash flow and free cash flow) and `get_balance_sheet` (total debt), each value with its `prior_year`; `research_tool_budget` 5 with `submit_note` exempt; a failed run gives that stock the note "research unavailable", unless a note was refused for length: that note is then kept, trimmed (`trim_note`, at a sentence), in place of the forced retry or the loop's "research unavailable"), then the bull and the bear, independently and from the same evidence, with no research tool (every stock rated low/medium/high; **fundamentals first, news as support**: the bull and the bear base each case on the stock's fundamentals and financial health, and the note and the news only support it. The bear names a concern and rates a stock HIGH only on evidence in its fundamentals: a decline in revenue or operating margin, a fall in free cash flow or a rise in debt on the fact sheet, OR a fact the company itself reports or files, found in the note (results, a guidance cut, a share issuance, going-concern doubts, a covenant breach, a restatement, an impairment). News from others (a competitor's technology, an analyst action, a lawsuit headline, speculation about the future) is never high by itself, whatever consequence the bear argues: there is no "lasting consequence" escape, in the bear's prompt or the judge's. When the fact sheet's fundamentals are unavailable or stale (a foreign filer with annual figures only), the company's own reported facts in the note are the evidence, so such a stock can still be rated high. "A fallen price with intact cash flow makes a company cheaper, not riskier." `momentum_exhaustion` needs a decline that ran for weeks: `drawdown_from_52w_high_pct` of -15 or below together with `return_1m_skip_1w_pct` of -10 or below (MU on 2026-03-31: -21.9% over the month but -20.4% of it in the last week, about -1.9% before it, so a one-week drop on a news story is no exhaustion; the Qwen 3.8 bear rated it high in 6 of 7 replays). The bull's prompt never mentions `momentum_exhaustion`. Every prompt carries the sources rule ("Sources can be wrong or stale: do not repeat a figure from a news item as fact when the fact sheet or a filing says otherwise"), except the no-news researcher's, which has no word of news; the researcher is told the fact sheet already gives the latest SEC figures against a year earlier, looks for what the company itself reported or filed and what explains the figures, and must not repeat the fact sheet's numbers; it says who reported each fact, the company or others, since the note is the bear's and judge's only channel for telling a company's own facts, where a high is allowed, from third-party news), then the judge (a news story from others, already priced or not, is no reason for out; a risk the bear shows in the fundamentals can be, by the same evidence rule; it also puts a stock out when its case does not earn a place or to leave room under `max_book_size`): a strength (strong / standard / weak: the bull's conviction against the bear's risk, rating the whole debate) and `in` or `out`, with a reason, on EVERY candidate; only a strong or standard stock can be `in` (a weak `in` is rejected, a weak stock is always out); at most `max_book_size` (10) `in`, a ceiling, not a target (10-23 run: Qwen 3.8 filled the book in 39 of 55 reviews and put holdings out for room), no veto cap; it sees its `previous_verdict` (verdict, reason, date and strength) on a stock it judged before and must give `what_changed` to flip the verdict (the submission is rejected otherwise; a change of strength alone, strong to standard say, needs none, while a held stock now rated weak goes out and so gives `what_changed`; a stock judged under a version-2 state file shows no strength), `recent_verdicts` (every `out` verdict of the last `feedback_reviews` (4) reviews that still has a price, with the stock's return since against that day's `in` stocks and its reason cut to 150 characters: the "then" close is the last session before the review's date in TODAY's loaded series (`DailySeries.dates`, `DailySeries.close_before`), the same adjustment basis as the latest close, so a split in paper/live (Yahoo bars are re-downloaded adjusted at every review) shows no fake return; the close stored with the verdict is the fallback; no price after `clock.now()`), and has `search_memory` and `remember_lesson` (`strategy.memory`, wiped at the start of every backtest; `judge_memory_budget` 3, `submit_verdicts` exempt). Code builds the book (`book.build_book`: every `in` stock, in rank order; no held-first rule), sizes it by the judge's strength alone (`sizing.strength_weights`: `strong_weight` 14%, `standard_weight` 9%, no weak weight since a weak stock is never in the book, no volatility term; the weights are scaled down by one factor when they sum above 98%, otherwise used as they are, and `target_portfolio` parks the rest in SHV as before, so ten standard stocks make 90% and 8% goes to SHV, zero `in` parks everything but the cash buffer; resizing on a strength change is the rebalancer's band, best effort: a scaled book can miss a change under 3 points, at no cost in exposure) and rebalances through `strategies/common/rebalancer.py`'s `Rebalancer`. A holding the judge puts out is sold and starts a cooldown of `reentry_cooldown_reviews` (4) completed reviews (`cooldown.advance_cooldowns`). No overlays. A bull, bear or judge with no valid submission after the forced retry (`ReviewPipeline._run_stage`: a run that submits nothing valid gets ONE more run with the submit tool forced; the warning for a run that ended without calling it names how it ended -- `finish_reason`, input, output, reasoning and total tokens (`(finish_reason=length, 7354 input, 12288 output, 12288 reasoning, 19642 total tokens)`), where the model reports them, a `length` stop with every output token spent on reasoning being a spent output cap (or window); the `submission rejected` warning carries the same detail, since a run can hit the window right after a rejection; each agent has its own submit tool as `final_tools`, so a recorded submission ends the run with no closing model turn; the researcher and the judge are created with `compact_history=True` (history summarized at 80% of `LLM_MAX_INPUT_TOKENS`, which `initialize` therefore requires: a missing one is a `FatalStrategyError`; once a judge run is compacted, its `memory` record in `reviews.jsonl` counts only the memory calls kept) -- and the INFO lines `[<agent>] retrying with <tool> forced` and `[<agent>] forced retry recorded <tool>` show the retry and that it landed (the retry is a fresh run, so `retry_prompt` repeats the stage's task, which carries the symbol and the character cap, and, for the researcher, says its research is gone: the note rests on the fact sheet alone and must say that no news or filings were checked, never that none were found); a retry that fails too logs its own warning and abandons the review), unusable data (`market_data.YahooBars`: one Yahoo batch in paper/live, fetched again after 60 s and 180 s; `GateBars` in backtests), or no candidate at all abandons the review with NO order, forced exits included, and stores nothing but the streak; a `BrokerError`/`BacktestError` abandons it too (stage `broker` before the rebalancer starts: no order was sent; stage `execution` during it: orders may already be out, logged in `reviews.jsonl` with the judge's verdicts, the book, the targets and the orders placed so far). An abandoned review stores only the streak, except at the `execution` stage, where the judge has decided and orders may be out: its verdicts and a cooldown for each of its sells actually placed (`cooldowns_started` in that log line lists those, none when no sell went out) are stored, running cooldowns are not counted down, no `history` entry is added and the day stays open, so a stock sold that week is not bought back by the next review. A backtest raises `FatalStrategyError` at 3 abandoned in a row (paper/live log and carry on). State in `data/bull_bear_state_<mode>.json` (`StateStore`, `STATE_VERSION` 3: last completed review date, abandoned streak, the judge's latest verdict per stock with its strength (`{verdict, reason, date, strength}`, `STRENGTHS` from `handoff.py`; a strength, when an entry has one, must be known and an `in` is never weak, else the file is invalid; an entry may have none, see the version-2 file), the cooldowns, the last `feedback_reviews` reviews' verdicts with their closes; a version-2 file (verdicts without strengths) loads whole, with no warning, and its verdicts show no previous strength until the stock is judged again (`StateStore.save` writes version 3 and keeps such a verdict as it is, strength-less, until the stock is judged again: no verdict is dropped, not even after an abandoned first review or for a stock the review did not debate); a version-1 file (picker or veto judge) keeps its review date and streak and loads the rest empty, so a completed Tuesday is not reviewed again; any other version or invalid content loads empty with a warning; wiped by `initialize` in backtests only). `ReviewLog` appends one line per review (forced exits included) to `reviews.jsonl` in the run directory: `verdicts` (each with `strength`, `what_changed` and `previous_verdict`), `verdict_counts`, `strength_counts`, `book`, `targets`, `cooldowns_active`, `cooldowns_started`, `recent_verdicts`, `memory` (calls and lessons), `notes` (`written`, `failed`, and `trimmed`: the stocks whose refused note was kept trimmed, counted in `written`); `dashboard/reviews_reader.py` reads `debate_set[].held`, `forced_exits`, `targets` and `orders` from `reviews.jsonl`, so those keys are kept. `research_news` (default on) is mirrored into `strategy.parameters`, so `settings.json` tells a news-off run apart; the news-off comparison run is made by setting `BullBearParams.research_news` to False (the strategy factory passes no `settings`, so the default in `parameters.py` is the switch: there is no command-line option). `AgentHandle.run` wraps every exception, `ConfigurationError` included, as `AgentError`, so a misconfiguration surfacing DURING a review costs only that stock's note ("research unavailable"), or its trimmed refused note when one was refused for length; the real misconfigurations (news provider when `research_news` is on, SEC user agent, `LLM_MODEL`) are caught at `initialize`.

- `bull_bear/batch/` (run by hand over finished runs; the strategy never imports it): `agent_scores.py` (**pure**: `forward_returns` from the open of the first session after each review, the fill, to the open 5 and 20 sessions later, with the excess over that review's candidate mean; a candidate without an open, or a review the calendar does not reach back to, is dropped and counted, and an exit past the last session is counted apart as immature; `score_agents`: the bull's high - low conviction, the bear's low - high risk with a concern breakdown, the judge's in - out (a picker record's `picks` are in, the rest of its debate set out), and the judge's strengths over every rated stock, in or out (strong - weak, then strong - standard; a run without strengths prints empty blocks), each spread computed per review and averaged, with a t-stat across reviews, Newey-West with 3 lags at 20 sessions; abandoned reviews count for nothing) and `scores_cli.py` (`uv run bull-bear-scores <run_dir>...`: reads `reviews.jsonl` itself, never the dashboard's reader, fetches opens through `YahooDailyBars` with SPY as the calendar, prints runs side by side; exit 2 without `reviews.jsonl`, 1 on a Yahoo failure).

## Gotchas

- **bull_bear logs its own order events.** `BullBearStrategy` overrides `on_new_order`, `on_partially_filled_order` and `on_filled_order` to log `new order: sell 7.842749 SYRE (market, id ...)` / `order filled: sell 7.842749 of 7.842749 SYRE @ $96.84 (id ...)`; the size is this fill's, against the order's. The base `Strategy` hooks and the executor stay silent on purpose: no other strategy logs these.

- **bull_bear fails fast without a news source (unless `research_news` is off), and its sectors are not point-in-time.** `BullBearStrategy.initialize` calls `_require_news_provider` when `research_news` is on (`broker.news_provider()` must exist) and turns any `ConfigurationError` (no news provider, no model, missing `SEC_EDGAR_USER_AGENT`) into `FatalStrategyError`, in EVERY mode: `ALPACA_NEWS_*` is required for paper/live and backtests alike while `research_news` is on, stricter than bill_ackman, whose news tool only fails when called (a backtest builds its Alpaca provider lazily, so without the check every note of the run would be research-free). The fact sheet's sector comes from yfinance live, even in a backtest (`strategies/common/sector_provider.py`, cached per run, `"UNKNOWN"` on a failure): the agents see today's sector, a known look-ahead on a label, not a price. Paper/live read daily bars from Yahoo through `market_data.YahooBars` (cross_momentum's reasoning: IEX volume would empty the $20M filter); a backtest reads the gate (`Strategy.get_historical_prices`) and `run_backtesting` preloads the universe, SHV and SPY in one Yahoo download. A forced exit is sold by `Rebalancer` only once the review reaches execution: a review abandoned before that sells nothing, so a data outage never liquidates the book.
- **Every agent's turns are bounded in three layers, and a run that fails is never corrected in place.** First the thinking budget: the researcher, bull and bear get `BullBearParams.thinking_token_budget` (28672) and the judge `judge_thinking_token_budget` (36864), sent to the vLLM server (`agents.create(thinking_token_budget=...)`, `agents/CLAUDE.md`), which ends a turn's reasoning there so the model goes on to its submit call. It is a safety net set high on purpose (the 10-25 run, effort `medium` in a 32k window, peaked at 11.6k output tokens (bear)): a healthy turn never reaches it, a budget below normal use would cut good reasoning, and a turn that does reach it logs a WARNING. Second the output cap, the backstop for an answer that runs on and for a server that ignores the budget: the same agents get `max_output_tokens` (36864) and the judge `judge_max_output_tokens` (45056), each its budget plus 8,192, the room left after a cut for the submission (one run per review covers every stock, up to about 25: the judge's reason up to 300 characters per stock plus `what_changed` up to 300 on each flip is about 15,000 characters, 4-5k tokens at most; see `agents/output_cap.py`). All four values are recorded in `settings.json`. `None` for either judge value means no budget (or no cap) for the judge, not the shared one. The window check (an ESTIMATE, to be measured by the replay): the judge's prompt reaches about 24k (about 20k before the fundamentals fields, which add roughly 3-4k to the bull, bear and judge contexts: about 14 fields x up to 25 stocks), so 24k + 45,056 is about 69k of the 81,920-token window (a prompt plus the cap above the window makes the cap's fallback send the call uncapped). Third compaction (researcher and judge, `compact_history=True`), whose summary call gets neither the budget nor the cap. A turn cut by the budget normally still submits, so it costs no forced retry (an answer that runs on after the cut can still reach the cap); a cap far above a healthy turn only makes each runaway turn cost more. A turn stopped at the cap with no tool call ends the run (`finish_reason=length`, nothing recorded), and so does a model that stops after a rejected submission; `_run_stage`'s forced retry then starts a FRESH run (except for a researcher whose note was refused for length, which keeps that note trimmed instead of a retry) with the corrective prompt, the submit tool forced and thinking on, and abandonment follows a second failure. Tried and reverted (10-19): `thinking.py`'s two middlewares, which asked the same turn again, or the turn after a rejection, with Qwen's thinking off and the submit tool forced, inside the run: the model re-sent the same rejected text (504, 504, 504 characters) or did not follow the tool sequence, so a correction in place did not work; do not re-add them. The one bound inside a run is `BullBearParams.max_agent_steps` (60 LangGraph supersteps; a tool call is a round trip of 3 and a run takes 3 x calls + 5 at worst, so the worst normal run, a researcher using its 5 tool calls, needs a limit of 21, and each rejected resubmission adds 3; see `agents/CLAUDE.md`), passed to every `AgentHandle.run(max_steps=...)`: a model that keeps resubmitting a rejected call (or whose forced call keeps failing) ends in `AgentLoopError`, and `_run_stage` logs an error (`run stopped as a loop`) and raises `AgentLooped` (a `ReviewAbandoned`) at once, with no forced retry (a fresh run of a model that just looped would only loop again). For the bull, bear and judge that abandons the review; for the researcher it costs only that stock its note ("research unavailable"), like any researcher failure, unless a note was refused for length first: that note is kept, trimmed. The hard limit on a note (next paragraph) removes the commonest rejection.
- **A note is asked for at `note_max_chars` (500) but refused only above `note_hard_max_chars` (600).** A model cannot count characters: in the 10-19 run 25 notes came back 1-20% over 500, and the model re-sent the same text when corrected, so the run ended and the pipeline's forced retry, a fresh run, wrote the note from the fact sheet alone: the news and filings the first run found were lost over a few characters. The prompt (`researcher_task`) and the retry prompt keep saying 500, and so does the recorder's refusal: it names 500, asks for about 450 by dropping the least important facts (not by trimming words), never quotes 600, and prefixes `this is the same text you sent before:` when the note is identical to the one last refused (10-23 run: Qwen 3.8 re-sent `601, 601, 601...`). `HandoffRecorder.refused_note` keeps the last note refused for length until the next `expect_*`.
