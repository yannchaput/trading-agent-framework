# strategies/bull_bear/ -- bull vs bear debate

> Nested `CLAUDE.md`; project-wide rules are in the root `CLAUDE.md`. Paths are relative to `src/trading_agent_framework/` unless they start with `tests/`, `scripts/` or `docs/`.

## Architecture

- `bull_bear/` (`BullBearStrategy` in `agent_bull_bear.py`, registered as `"bull_bear"`): lumibot's bull vs bear debate over cross_momentum's ranking, once a week (Tuesday 12:00 ET, `sleeptime = "1D"`, `BullBearParams.rebalance_weekday` = 1: every other session returns at once; a Tuesday whose review completed is not run again after a restart, unlike cross_momentum). Code ranks the universe with `strategies/common/scoring.py` and cross_momentum's own `CONFIG` (never duplicated) and builds the candidates (`debate_set.py`): the top `candidate_count` (15) plus the holdings ranked up to `retention_rank` (35), minus the stocks in their re-entry cooldown (`max_volatility` gates new entries only: the pipeline scores a holding with a copy of `CONFIG` that lifts it, so `scoring.py`/cross_momentum are untouched; a holding ranked worse, unranked or still in cooldown is a forced exit, sold by code, never shown to the agents, and logged as a warning (`forced exit: <symbol> (<reason>)`) just before its sell order is sent: not when the review is abandoned before execution, since nothing is sold then). The fact sheet (`fact_sheet.py`) has NO `held` or `weight_pct`: no agent is told what is held. It carries `company`, the SEC `entityName`, because the model otherwise guesses a company from the ticker when a search finds no news (the 2026-10-30 2Y run: UI as an insurer, EMBJ as an ETF and put out for it, at least 71 of 1,759 notes); it changes no search, since `search_news` filters on tickers only. Beside the momentum figures it carries `return_1m_skip_1w_pct` (the month before the last week: `compute_return_from_prices(closes, 16, 5)`, so a stock that ran up this week is told apart from one that was already strong) and the SEC fundamentals (`fundamentals.py`: `SecFundamentals`, growth, margins, cash flow, debt, current assets and liabilities and `runway_months`, money in billions of the REPORTING currency labelled `currency`, a figure that cannot be computed left out; the current assets and liabilities are there because the SEC `cash` tags leave out marketable securities, which made the agents' runway reasoning wrong: in the 2026-10-30 2Y run IOVA went out on a "<6mo runway" from $111M of cash with $464M of current assets, 127 of the 182 measurable cash-based outs had current assets of at least 1.5x cash, and a debt-free stock with no revenue, SYRE, read `no SEC figures` at every review; `runway_months` is current assets less current liabilities over the monthly burn (free cash flow, else operating cash flow), absent when no cash is burning, and code computes it because a prompt line alone failed: asked in words to use the two figures, the replayed bear and judge still divided the note's cash by the burn; `BALANCE_SHEET_TAGS["cash"]` stays as it is, bill_ackman imports it), read by code for EVERY candidate as of `strategy.clock.now()` (never the wall clock) through the one `SecEdgarClient` `initialize` builds with `default_client` and shares with the researcher's SEC tools (framework 1-day cache). **Fundamentals count only filings dated BEFORE the review day**, in every mode: company facts carry `filed` as a date only (read as midnight), so `SecFundamentals` reads the statements (and the staleness test) as of the start of `as_of`'s calendar day (in its own timezone) minus 1 microsecond; a 10-Q filed on the review day, often after the close, stays hidden (in paper/live a same-morning filing shows up the next day). The client call still gets the real `as_of` (cache freshness only, no `max_age_days`). The researcher's SEC tools still use `filed <= as_of`, so a tool call can show a same-day filing that the fact sheet does not. The first backtest downloads each debated stock's company facts once (about 4 MB each, several hundred MB of cache in total); later runs and reviews reuse the files. Any SEC failure, or an exception from the injected `fundamentals_of`, gives that stock `fundamentals_unavailable` (the reason; `"error"` for an unexpected exception, with a WARNING naming the symbol) and never abandons the review; `fact_sheet_row` stays pure (the pipeline reads, the sheet merges the fields after `sector`). Four agents hand over through submit tools validated by `HandoffRecorder(SubmitRecorder)` (`handoff.py`, `HandoffError` and the readers from `common/handoff.py`, `NoteTooLong` subclasses it; `_covering` is `common.covering` with bull_bear's messages: `submit_note`, `submit_bull_case`, `submit_bear_case`, `submit_verdicts`; no `from __future__ import annotations`): a researcher run PER STOCK (`search_news` when `research_news` is on, pinned to the stock being researched by `pinned_news.pin_search_news`: a `symbols` with no uppercase ticker in it (empty, a company name, a lower-case ticker) searches the stock whose note is expected (`HandoffRecorder.note_symbol`) and the result says `symbols_searched`, a name among tickers is dropped, another stock's ticker passes; the shared tool reads an empty `symbols` as market-wide news and the model guesses names for tickers it does not know: 134 of 2,170 calls in the 2026-10-30 2Y run, and the prompt says to pass the ticker; plus the SEC `get_income_statement` (with cash flow and free cash flow) and `get_balance_sheet` (total debt), each value with its `prior_year`; `research_tool_budget` 5 with `submit_note` exempt; a failed run gives that stock the note "research unavailable", unless a note was refused for length: that note is then kept, trimmed (`trim_note`, at a sentence), in place of the forced retry or the loop's "research unavailable"), then the bull and the bear, independently and from the same evidence, with no research tool (every stock rated low/medium/high; **fundamentals first, news as support**: the bull and the bear base each case on the stock's fundamentals and financial health, and the note and the news only support it. The bear names a concern and rates a stock HIGH only on evidence in its fundamentals: a decline in revenue or operating margin, a fall in free cash flow or a rise in debt on the fact sheet, OR a fact the company itself reports or files, found in the note (results, a guidance cut, a share issuance, going-concern doubts, a covenant breach, a restatement, an impairment). News from others (a competitor's technology, an analyst action, a lawsuit headline, speculation about the future) is never high by itself, whatever consequence the bear argues: there is no "lasting consequence" escape, in the bear's prompt or the judge's. When the fact sheet's fundamentals are unavailable or stale (a foreign filer with annual figures only), the company's own reported facts in the note are the evidence, so such a stock can still be rated high. "A fallen price with intact cash flow makes a company cheaper, not riskier." `momentum_exhaustion` needs a decline that ran for weeks: `drawdown_from_52w_high_pct` of -15 or below together with `return_1m_skip_1w_pct` of -10 or below (MU on 2026-03-31: -21.9% over the month but -20.4% of it in the last week, about -1.9% before it, so a one-week drop on a news story is no exhaustion; the Qwen 3.8 bear rated it high in 6 of 7 replays). The bull's prompt never mentions `momentum_exhaustion`. Every prompt carries the sources rule ("Sources can be wrong or stale: do not repeat a figure from a news item as fact when the fact sheet or a filing says otherwise"), except the no-news researcher's, which has no word of news; the researcher is told the fact sheet already gives the latest SEC figures against a year earlier, looks for what the company itself reported or filed and what explains the figures, and must not repeat the fact sheet's numbers; it says who reported each fact, the company or others, since the note is the bear's and judge's only channel for telling a company's own facts, where a high is allowed, from third-party news), then the judge (a news story from others, already priced or not, is no reason for out; a risk the bear shows in the fundamentals can be, by the same evidence rule; it also puts a stock out when its case does not earn a place or to leave room under `max_book_size`): a strength (strong / standard / weak: the bull's conviction against the bear's risk, rating the whole debate) and `in` or `out`, with a reason, on EVERY candidate; only a strong or standard stock can be `in` (a weak `in` is rejected, a weak stock is always out); at most `max_book_size` (10) `in`, a ceiling, not a target (10-23 run: Qwen 3.8 filled the book in 39 of 55 reviews and put holdings out for room), no veto cap; it sees its `previous_verdict` (verdict, reason, date and strength) on a stock it judged before and must give `what_changed` to flip the verdict (the submission is rejected otherwise; a change of strength alone, strong to standard say, needs none, while a held stock now rated weak goes out and so gives `what_changed`; a stock judged under a version-2 state file shows no strength), and has `search_memory` and `remember_lesson` (`strategy.memory`, wiped at the start of every backtest; it holds only the lessons the judge records, no verdict or reason: those are in the state file and `reviews.jsonl`, and the judge sees a past `in` reason only as the `previous_verdict` of a stock it is judging again; `judge_memory_budget` 3, `submit_verdicts` exempt). It is shown no outcomes of its past verdicts: `recent_verdicts`, the out verdicts of its last 4 reviews with their returns since, was removed on 2026-10-31. An 18-review replay without it and without the lessons it had produced left in - out no worse (-0.32 at 20 sessions, t -0.25; +0.47 at 5 sessions, t 0.67) for 24% fewer input tokens, 38% less reasoning and 33% less time per review, although it did change verdicts beyond rerun noise (33 of 289 against 20); listing the `in` verdicts too had taken it to about 64 entries a review and was reverted first, with 2 judge stops in 18 reviews against none in 54 calls after. Do not re-add it without a test that shows a gain. Code builds the book (`book.build_book`: every `in` stock, in rank order; no held-first rule), sizes it with equal weights (`sizing.equal_weights`: `stock_weight` 9.8% for every stock, whatever its strength; no volatility term; the weight is fixed, never scaled, so a full book of `max_book_size` (10) makes 98% and a shorter one leaves the rest to `target_portfolio`, which parks it in SHV, zero `in` parks everything but the cash buffer; `max_book_size x stock_weight` must fit in what is investable, and a strength change alone trades nothing. Why equal: on the same books over the 1Y window (2026-10-29 study, the final-prompt judge's books in 2 replayed runs and the 11 older runs' books), equal weights beat sizing by strength, 14% strong / 9% standard (no edge, more turnover; its 14-review backtest trailed the older runs), and capped inverse volatility (behind in 10 of 11 runs); the strength stays a selection rule (a weak stock is out) and is still logged and scored) and rebalances through `strategies/common/rebalancer.py`'s `Rebalancer`. A holding the judge puts out is sold and starts a cooldown of `reentry_cooldown_reviews` (4) completed reviews (`common/cooldowns.py`, `advance_cooldowns`, shared with bill_ackman). No overlays. A bull, bear or judge with no valid submission after the forced retry (`BullBearPipeline._run_stage`: a run that submits nothing valid gets ONE more run with the submit tool forced; the warning for a run that ended without calling it names how it ended -- `finish_reason`, input, output, reasoning and total tokens (`(finish_reason=length, 7354 input, 12288 output, 12288 reasoning, 19642 total tokens)`), where the model reports them, a `length` stop with every output token spent on reasoning being a spent output cap (or window); the `submission rejected` warning carries the same detail, since a run can hit the window right after a rejection; each agent has its own submit tool as `final_tools`, so a recorded submission ends the run with no closing model turn; the researcher and the judge are created with `compact_history=True` (history summarized at 80% of `LLM_MAX_INPUT_TOKENS`, which `initialize` therefore requires: a missing one is a `FatalStrategyError`; once a judge run is compacted, its `memory` record in `reviews.jsonl` counts only the memory calls kept) -- and the INFO lines `[<agent>] retrying with <tool> forced` and `[<agent>] forced retry recorded <tool>` show the retry and that it landed (the retry is a fresh run, so `retry_prompt` repeats the stage's task, which carries the symbol and the character cap, and, for the researcher, says its research is gone: the note rests on the fact sheet alone and must say that no news or filings were checked, never that none were found); a retry that fails too logs its own warning and abandons the review), unusable data (`market_data.YahooBars`: one Yahoo batch in paper/live through `common/yahoo_daily_bars.fetch_usable_batch`, fetched again after 60 s and 180 s, `UnusableBatch` becoming `DataUnavailable`; `GateBars` in backtests), or no candidate at all abandons the review with NO order, forced exits included, and stores nothing but the streak; a `BrokerError`/`BacktestError` abandons it too (stage `broker` before the rebalancer starts: no order was sent; stage `execution` during it: orders may already be out, logged in `reviews.jsonl` with the judge's verdicts, the book, the targets and the orders placed so far). An abandoned review stores only the streak, except at the `execution` stage, where the judge has decided and orders may be out: its verdicts and a cooldown for each of its sells actually placed (`cooldowns_started` in that log line lists those, none when no sell went out) are stored, running cooldowns are not counted down and the day stays open, so a stock sold that week is not bought back by the next review. A backtest raises `FatalStrategyError` at 3 abandoned in a row (paper/live log and carry on). State in `data/bull_bear_state_<mode>.json` (`StateStore(JsonStateStore)`, `STATE_VERSION` 3, `accepts` {1, 2, 3}: last completed review date, abandoned streak, the judge's latest verdict per stock with its strength (`{verdict, reason, date, strength}`, `STRENGTHS` from `handoff.py`; a strength, when an entry has one, must be known and an `in` is never weak, else the file is invalid; an entry may have none, see the version-2 file) and the cooldowns; a file that still carries the old `history` block loads whole and the next save drops it; a version-2 file (verdicts without strengths) loads whole, with no warning, and its verdicts show no previous strength until the stock is judged again (`StateStore.save` writes version 3 and keeps such a verdict as it is, strength-less, until the stock is judged again: no verdict is dropped, not even after an abandoned first review or for a stock the review did not debate); a version-1 file (picker or veto judge) keeps its review date and streak and loads the rest empty, so a completed Tuesday is not reviewed again; any other version or invalid content loads empty with a warning; wiped by `initialize` in backtests only, through `fresh=`). A `RunLog` (`common/run_log.py`) appends one line per review (forced exits included) to `reviews.jsonl` in the run directory: `verdicts` (each with `strength`, `what_changed` and `previous_verdict`), `verdict_counts`, `strength_counts`, `book`, `targets`, `cooldowns_active`, `cooldowns_started`, `memory` (calls and lessons), `notes` (`written`, `failed`, and `trimmed`: the stocks whose refused note was kept trimmed, counted in `written`); `dashboard/reviews_reader.py` reads `debate_set[].held`, `forced_exits`, `targets` and `orders` from `reviews.jsonl`, so those keys are kept. `research_news` (default on) is mirrored into `strategy.parameters`, so `settings.json` tells a news-off run apart; the news-off comparison run is made by setting `BullBearParams.research_news` to False (the strategy factory passes no `settings`, so the default in `parameters.py` is the switch: there is no command-line option). `AgentHandle.run` wraps every exception, `ConfigurationError` included, as `AgentError`, so a misconfiguration surfacing DURING a review costs only that stock's note ("research unavailable"), or its trimmed refused note when one was refused for length; the real misconfigurations (news provider when `research_news` is on, SEC user agent, `LLM_MODEL`) are caught at `initialize`.

- `bull_bear/batch/` (run by hand over finished runs; the strategy never imports it): `agent_scores.py` (**pure**: `forward_returns` from the open of the first session after each review, the fill, to the open 5 and 20 sessions later, with the excess over that review's candidate mean; a candidate without an open, or a review the calendar does not reach back to, is dropped and counted, and an exit past the last session is counted apart as immature; `score_agents`: the bull's high - low conviction, the bear's low - high risk with a concern breakdown, the judge's in - out (a picker record's `picks` are in, the rest of its debate set out), and the judge's strengths over every rated stock, in or out (strong - weak, then strong - standard; a run without strengths prints empty blocks), each spread computed per review and averaged, with a t-stat across reviews, Newey-West with 3 lags at 20 sessions; abandoned reviews count for nothing) and `scores_cli.py` (`uv run bull-bear-scores <run_dir>...`: reads `reviews.jsonl` itself, never the dashboard's reader, fetches opens through `YahooDailyBars` with SPY as the calendar, prints runs side by side; exit 2 without `reviews.jsonl`, 1 on a Yahoo failure).

## Gotchas

- **bull_bear backtests debate stocks from the universe as it stood on each date, not today's file.** Same pre-step, history file, cache and errors as cross_momentum (`strategies/cross_momentum/CLAUDE.md`): `run_backtesting` calls `ensure_universe_history(start, end)` first (`UniverseHistoryError` stops the run before the runner starts; the window's symbols come from `window_symbols`, each review's list from `month_universe`, both in `strategies/common/universe_history.py`), records `parameters["universe"]` (`mode: point_in_time`, the pool counts and `preload_missing`) and preloads the window's union through `UniverseBacktestData` (batched, retried, raising above 1% empty); each review ranks `history.on(review date)` minus SHV through `BullBearPipeline(universe_on=...)`, and a holding that fell out of that month's list is unranked, so the existing forced exit sells it. A month missing from the history raises `FatalStrategyError`. The factory passes no `universe` in backtests (`point_in_time=True`); paper/live keep the file and refuse an empty universe (`ConfigurationError`). **Residual bias: stocks delisted inside the window are absent from every list.**
- **bull_bear logs its own order events.** `BullBearStrategy` overrides `on_new_order`, `on_partially_filled_order` and `on_filled_order` to log `new order: sell 7.842749 SYRE (market, id ...)` / `order filled: sell 7.842749 of 7.842749 SYRE @ $96.84 (id ...)`; the size is this fill's, against the order's. The base `Strategy` hooks and the executor stay silent on purpose: no other strategy logs these.

- **bull_bear fails fast without a news source (unless `research_news` is off), and its sectors are not point-in-time.** `BullBearStrategy.initialize` calls `_require_news_provider` when `research_news` is on (`broker.news_provider()` must exist) and turns any `ConfigurationError` (no news provider, no model, missing `SEC_EDGAR_USER_AGENT`) into `FatalStrategyError`, in EVERY mode: `ALPACA_NEWS_*` is required for paper/live and backtests alike while `research_news` is on, stricter than bill_ackman, whose news tool only fails when called (a backtest builds its Alpaca provider lazily, so without the check every note of the run would be research-free). The fact sheet's sector comes from yfinance live, even in a backtest (`strategies/common/sector_provider.py`, cached per run, `"UNKNOWN"` on a failure): the agents see today's sector, a known look-ahead on a label, not a price. Paper/live read daily bars from Yahoo through `market_data.YahooBars` (cross_momentum's reasoning: IEX volume would empty the $20M filter); a backtest reads the gate (`Strategy.get_historical_prices`) and `run_backtesting` preloads the universe, SHV and SPY in batched Yahoo downloads (`UniverseBacktestData`). A forced exit is sold by `Rebalancer` only once the review reaches execution: a review abandoned before that sells nothing, so a data outage never liquidates the book.
- **Every agent's turns are bounded in three layers, and a run that fails is never corrected in place.** First the thinking budget: the researcher, bull and bear get `BullBearParams.thinking_token_budget` (28672) and the judge `judge_thinking_token_budget` (36864), sent to the vLLM server (`agents.create(thinking_token_budget=...)`, `agents/CLAUDE.md`), which ends a turn's reasoning there so the model goes on to its submit call. It is a safety net set high on purpose (the 10-25 run, effort `medium` in a 32k window, peaked at 11.6k output tokens (bear)): a healthy turn never reaches it, a budget below normal use would cut good reasoning, and a turn that does reach it logs a WARNING. Second the output cap, the backstop for an answer that runs on and for a server that ignores the budget: the same agents get `max_output_tokens` (36864) and the judge `judge_max_output_tokens` (45056), each its budget plus 8,192, the room left after a cut for the submission (one run per review covers every stock, up to about 25: the judge's reason up to 300 characters per stock plus `what_changed` up to 300 on each flip is about 15,000 characters, 4-5k tokens at most; see `agents/output_cap.py`). All four values are recorded in `settings.json`. `None` for either judge value means no budget (or no cap) for the judge, not the shared one. The window check (an ESTIMATE, to be measured by the replay): the judge's prompt reaches about 24k (about 20k before the fundamentals fields, which add roughly 3-4k to the bull, bear and judge contexts: about 14 fields x up to 25 stocks), so 24k + 45,056 is about 69k of the 81,920-token window (a prompt plus the cap above the window makes the cap's fallback send the call uncapped). Third compaction (researcher and judge, `compact_history=True`), whose summary call gets neither the budget nor the cap. A turn cut by the budget normally still submits, so it costs no forced retry (an answer that runs on after the cut can still reach the cap); a cap far above a healthy turn only makes each runaway turn cost more. A turn stopped at the cap with no tool call ends the run (`finish_reason=length`, nothing recorded), and so does a model that stops after a rejected submission; `_run_stage`'s forced retry then starts a FRESH run (except for a researcher whose note was refused for length, which keeps that note trimmed instead of a retry) with the corrective prompt, the submit tool forced and thinking on, and abandonment follows a second failure. Tried and reverted (10-19): `thinking.py`'s two middlewares, which asked the same turn again, or the turn after a rejection, with Qwen's thinking off and the submit tool forced, inside the run: the model re-sent the same rejected text (504, 504, 504 characters) or did not follow the tool sequence, so a correction in place did not work; do not re-add them. The one bound inside a run is `BullBearParams.max_agent_steps` (60 LangGraph supersteps; a tool call is a round trip of 3 and a run takes 3 x calls + 5 at worst, so the worst normal run, a researcher using its 5 tool calls, needs a limit of 21, and each rejected resubmission adds 3; see `agents/CLAUDE.md`), passed to every `AgentHandle.run(max_steps=...)`: a model that keeps resubmitting a rejected call (or whose forced call keeps failing) ends in `AgentLoopError`, and `_run_stage` logs an error (`run stopped as a loop`) and raises `AgentLooped` (a `StageAbandoned`) at once, with no forced retry (a fresh run of a model that just looped would only loop again). For the bull, bear and judge that abandons the review; for the researcher it costs only that stock its note ("research unavailable"), like any researcher failure, unless a note was refused for length first: that note is kept, trimmed. The hard limit on a note (next paragraph) removes the commonest rejection.
- **A note is asked for at `note_max_chars` (500) but refused only above `note_hard_max_chars` (700).** A model cannot count characters: in the 10-19 run 25 notes came back 1-20% over 500, and the model re-sent the same text when corrected, so the run ended and the pipeline's forced retry, a fresh run, wrote the note from the fact sheet alone: the news and filings the first run found were lost over a few characters. The prompt (`researcher_task`) and the retry prompt keep saying 500, and so does the recorder's refusal: it names 500, asks for about 450 by dropping the least important facts (not by trimming words), never quotes 700, and prefixes `this is the same text you sent before:` when the note is identical to the one last refused (10-23 run: Qwen 3.8 re-sent `601, 601, 601...`). `HandoffRecorder.refused_note` keeps the last note refused for length until the next `expect_*`. The limit went from 600 to 700 after the 2026-10-31 2Y run: first drafts were 22 characters longer than in the previous run (more articles from the `search_news` pin, the company name in the opening), 11.8% were refused against 7.7%, 12 runs looped against 8, and a refused note lost 133 characters of findings on average. Widen the limit, never the ask: the model aims at the ask (median first draft 495), so raising `note_max_chars` moves the drafts up with it.
- **Normal noise in a bull_bear backtest log (do not alert on it).** Per review, about one `tool budget of 5 calls spent: search_news was refused` (the researcher's sixth search); one researcher note-length loop (`run stopped as a loop`, the note kept trimmed) per 15 to 20 reviews; a judge or researcher forced retry or a thinking-budget hit a couple of times per 80 reviews. Yahoo sector rate limits print `Traceback` lines at DEBUG (sector `UNKNOWN`), so monitor `| ERROR |`, not `Traceback`. Real trouble is `review abandoned`, `finish_reason=length`, `too long to summarize` or a repeated forced retry.
- **Agent prompts must not contain `held` or `weight_pct`, and no prompt (system or task) may contain `forced`.** `test_no_prompt_explains_held_or_weight` and a sibling test enforce it: a sentence like "a position held" fails, so write around it.
- **A judge replay needs a rerun arm.** The same inputs rerun give different verdicts on about 7% of stocks (20 of 289 in the 2026-10-31 `recent_verdicts` replay) and reasoning of 6k to 13k tokens, so score the change against that noise, not against the logged run alone, and fix the keep/drop rule before looking at results.
